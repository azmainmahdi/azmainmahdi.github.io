Dark patterns in AI-generated interfaces: how freelance designers in the Global South evaluate and deliver them

Dark patterns are design tricks that push people into choices they did not mean to make. Freelance designers and developers in Bangladesh now use AI tools to build the interfaces they deliver to paying clients. We study how they notice these patterns in AI output, how they judge them, and what they change or send anyway.

Five AI-generated interfacesSubscription, streaming, e-commerce, travel and fitness, with ten dark patterns mapped to Gray et al.'s (2024) ontology
Task-based interviewEach participant reviews the screens as a client deliverable, decides what to change or send, then reflects on the patterns
Reflexive thematic analysisBangla-English transcripts, coded inductively from participants' own accounts of client work

ProgressThirteen interviews since September 2026 with freelancers who have recent paid client work. Recruitment is still open.

Signals of nutrition: a mixed-methods study of consumer perception

Interviews first, to learn how young consumers define "nutrition". Then a survey, to test those ideas at scale.

Ten interviewsSemi-structured, with thematic analysis
Surveyn = 202, sized with Cochran's formula; instrument validated at Cronbach's α = 0.90
ModelsMultiple regression, a multi-attribute attitude model and Spearman correlation, in Excel and Python

ResultThe attribute participants said mattered most did not significantly predict their perception. We flagged that gap for further research.
